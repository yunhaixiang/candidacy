\subsection{Motivations}\label{subsec:motivations}

\textit{Monodromy groups} are interesting mathematical objects that arise naturally in both geometric and arithmetic contexts. 
Roughly speaking, they describe how locally defined data change when transported along a loop. 
Controlling and understanding monodromy groups has been a central theme in both of these worlds, with significant applications.

The goal of this report is to provide an exposition on monodromy groups as well as ways to study them using methods from perverse sheaves and their middle convolution introduced by Katz \cite{Katz1996}, especially in the $\ell$-adic arithmetic context.

\parlabel{par:square-root-monodromy}Consider the equation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:square-root}
z(w)^2=w
\end{equation}
for the unknown holomorphic function $z:V\rightarrow\mathbb C^\times$, where $V:\mathsf{Open}(\mathbb C^\times)$ and $w:V$ is the independent variable. Fix a base point $w_0:\mathbb C^\times$ and choose $z_0:\mathbb C^\times$ with $z_0^2=w_0$.
On a sufficiently small disc $V$ containing $w_0$, the equation has two holomorphic solution branches $z_+(w)=\sqrt{w}$ and $z_-(w)=-\sqrt{w}$ with $z_+(w_0)=z_0$ and $z_-(w_0)=-z_0$. Move $w$ once around $0$ along the loop
$$\gamma:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb C^\times,\qquad t\mapsto w_0e^{2\pi i t}.$$
Along this loop, the values of the two solution branches vary continuously as $\widetilde\gamma_+(t)=z_0e^{\pi i t}$ and $\widetilde\gamma_-(t)=-z_0e^{\pi i t}$, until they eventually swap places at $t=1$.  
In other words, there is a permutation representation of the fundamental group\footnote{with the discrete topology} $\pi^{\mathrm{top}}_1(\mathbb C^\times,w_0)\cong\mathbb Z:\mathsf{Grp}^{\mathrm{top}}$
$$\rho:\pi^{\mathrm{top}}_1(\mathbb C^\times,w_0)\rightarrow\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathsf{Set}}(\{[z_+]_{w_0},[z_-]_{w_0}\}),\qquad n\mapsto \bigl([z_+]_{w_0}\ [z_-]_{w_0}\bigr)^n.$$
We call this representation the \textit{permutation monodromy representation}, and we call its image, isomorphic to $\mathrm{S}_2$, the \textit{permutation monodromy group}, of the equation (\cref{eq:square-root}).

\parlabel{par:square-root-sheaf}We reformulate the above with sheaves. Consider the covering
$$f:\mathbb C^\times\rightarrow\mathbb C^\times,\qquad \zeta\mapsto\zeta^2.$$
Let $\mathcal F:\mathsf{Sh}(\mathbb C^\times,\mathsf{Set})$ be its sheaf of holomorphic sections. Equivalently, this is the sheaf of solutions to the equation \eqref{eq:square-root},
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal F(V)&=\{z:V\to\mathbb C^\times\mid z\text{ holomorphic and }(f\circ z)|^V=\mathrm{id}_V\}\\
&=\{z:V\to\mathbb C^\times\mid z\text{ holomorphic and }z(w)^2=w\text{ for every }w:V\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
As in \cref{par:square-root-monodromy}, on a sufficiently small disc $V$ containing $w_0$, the sections are precisely the two holomorphic solution branches $z_+$ and $z_-$, identifying 
$$\mathcal F|_V\cong\underline{\{+,-\}}_V.$$
Hence $\mathcal F$ is locally constant, with stalk at $w_0$
$$\mathcal F_{w_0}=\{[z_+]_{w_0},[z_-]_{w_0}\}\cong f^{-1}(w_0)$$ 
identified by evaluation. 
However, the sheaf is not constant: $\mathcal F(\mathbb C^\times)=\emptyset$. A global section would determine a germ at $w_0$ fixed by all monodromy, whereas transport once around $0$ exchanges the two square-root germs, as demonstrated in \cref{par:square-root-monodromy}. 
Locally constant (but not necessarily constant) sheaves of sets, such as this one, are known as \textit{local systems of sets}. 


\parlabel{par:square-root-transport}For a path $\gamma:[0,1]\to\mathbb C^\times$ from $w_0=\gamma(0)$ to $w_1=\gamma(1)$ and a point $\zeta:f^{-1}(w_0)$ in the fibre, let $\widetilde\gamma_\zeta$ be the unique lift through $f$ satisfying $\widetilde\gamma_\zeta(0)=\zeta$.
We have the \textit{transport map}
\[
T_\gamma:\mathcal F_{w_0}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal F_{w_1},\qquad \zeta\longmapsto\widetilde\gamma_\zeta(1).
\]
with the evaluation identifications $\mathcal F_w\cong f^{-1}(w)$.
Thus we obtain a \textit{transport functor}\footnote{Here $\Pi_1(X)$ denotes the fundamental groupoid of some $X:\mathsf{Top}$, that is, a category whose objects are points of $X$ and whose morphisms are homotopy classes of paths relative to their endpoints, with concatenation as composition.}
\[
T:\Pi_1(\mathbb C^\times)\longrightarrow\mathsf{Set},\qquad w\longmapsto\mathcal F_w,\qquad [\gamma]\longmapsto T_\gamma.
\]
Restricting transport to loops at $w_0$ gives the same representation in \cref{par:square-root-monodromy}
$$\rho:\pi_1^{\mathrm{top}}(\mathbb C^\times,w_0)\rightarrow\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathsf{Set}}(\mathcal F_{w_0}),\qquad [\gamma]\mapsto T_\gamma.$$

In \cref{sec:local-systems}, we will see how to upgrade permutation monodromy representations into linear monodromy representations, which we will just call monodromy representations. 
Moreover, one can use this machinery to talk about monodromy in the $\ell$-adic arithmetic context, by replacing $\pi_1^{\mathrm{top}}$ with the étale fundamental group $\pi_1^{\text{ét}}$. 
When $X=\mathrm{Spec}(k)$ for some $k:\mathrm{Field}$, with a choice of separable closure $k^{\mathrm{sep}}$, then $\pi_1(X,\overline{x})=\mathrm{Gal}(k^{\mathrm{sep}}/k)$, where $x=\mathrm{Spec}(k^{\mathrm{sep}})$, is the absolute Galois group of $k$. Hence, a monodromy representation in this case degenerates into a Galois representation, a ubiquitous arithmetic object.

\subsection{Complex Local Systems}



We have looked at permutation monodromy, but permutation actions do not capture all the situations we wish to study, since a lot of times in nature we encounter actions on vector spaces instead of sets. 
For example, instead of an algebraic equation like the one in \cref{par:square-root-monodromy}, we consider a differential equation, which is known as \textit{Gauss's hypergeometric equation}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gauss-hypergeometric}
\lambda(1-\lambda)\frac{\mathrm d^2 f}{\mathrm d\lambda^2}
+\bigl(c-(a+b+1)\lambda\bigr)\frac{\mathrm d f}{\mathrm d\lambda}
-abf=0
\end{equation}
where $a,b,c:\mathbb C$ are fixed parameters and $f:V\rightarrow\mathbb C$ is an unknown holomorphic function, with $V:\mathsf{Open}(U)$, $U=\mathbb P^1(\mathbb C)\setminus\{0,1,\infty\}$, and independent variable $\lambda:V$.
We then define the sheaf of solutions to this equation $\mathcal F:\mathsf{Sh}(U,\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb C})$
\[
\mathcal F(V)=\left\{f:V\rightarrow\mathbb C\;\middle|\;f\text{ holomorphic and satisfies \eqref{eq:gauss-hypergeometric} on }V\right\}.
\]
This is a sheaf of vector spaces since \eqref{eq:gauss-hypergeometric} is a homogeneous linear differential equation. For each $\lambda_0:U$, the existence-and-uniqueness theorem \cite[Theorem~4.3.1]{HottaTakeuchiTanisaki2008} gives a sufficiently small open disc $V:\mathsf{Open}(U)$ containing $\lambda_0$ and an isomorphism of sheaves
\[
\mathcal F|_V\xrightarrow{\sim}\underline{\mathbb C}_V^2
\]
Thus $\mathcal F$ is locally constant of rank $2$. We call such a sheaf a \textit{complex local system of rank $2$}.

As in \cref{par:square-root-transport}, we have a \textit{transport functor}
$$T:\Pi_1(U)\rightarrow\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb C}^{\mathrm{fin}},\qquad \lambda\mapsto\mathcal F_\lambda,\qquad [\gamma]\mapsto T_\gamma,$$
