\subsection{Motivations}\label{subsec:motivations}

\textit{Monodromy groups} are interesting mathematical objects that arise naturally in both geometric and arithmetic contexts. 
Roughly speaking, they describe how locally defined data change when transported along a loop. 
Controlling and understanding monodromy groups has been a central theme in both of these worlds, with significant applications.

The goal of this report is to provide an exposition on monodromy groups as well as ways to study them using methods from perverse sheaves and their middle convolution introduced by Katz \cite{Katz1996}, especially in the $\ell$-adic arithmetic context.

\parlabel{par:square-root-monodromy}Consider the equation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:square-root}
z(u)^2=u
\end{equation}
for the unknown holomorphic function $z:V\rightarrow\mathbb C^\times$, where $V:\mathsf{Open}(\mathbb C^\times)$ and $u:V$ is the independent variable. Fix a base point $u_0:\mathbb C^\times$ and choose $z_0:\mathbb C^\times$ with $z_0^2=u_0$.
On a sufficiently small disc $V$ containing $u_0$, the equation has two holomorphic solution branches $z_+(u)=\sqrt{u}$ and $z_-(u)=-\sqrt{u}$ with $z_+(u_0)=z_0$ and $z_-(u_0)=-z_0$. Move $u$ once around $0$ along the loop
$$\gamma:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb C^\times,\qquad t\mapsto u_0e^{2\pi i t}.$$
Along this loop, the values of the two solution branches vary continuously as $\widetilde\gamma_+(t)=z_0e^{\pi i t}$ and $\widetilde\gamma_-(t)=-z_0e^{\pi i t}$, until they eventually swap places at $t=1$.  
In other words, there is a permutation representation of the fundamental group\footnote{with the discrete topology} $\pi^{\mathrm{top}}_1(\mathbb C^\times,u_0)\cong\mathbb Z:\mathsf{Grp}^{\mathrm{top}}$
$$\rho:\pi^{\mathrm{top}}_1(\mathbb C^\times,u_0)\rightarrow\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathsf{Set}}(\{[z_+]_{u_0},[z_-]_{u_0}\}),\qquad n\mapsto \bigl([z_+]_{u_0}\ [z_-]_{u_0}\bigr)^n.$$
We call this representation the \textit{permutation monodromy representation}, and we call its image, isomorphic to $\mathrm{S}_2$, the \textit{permutation monodromy group}, of the equation (\cref{eq:square-root}) at $u_0$.

\parlabel{par:square-root-sheaf}We reformulate the above with sheaves. Consider the covering
$$f:\mathbb C^\times\rightarrow\mathbb C^\times,\qquad \zeta\mapsto\zeta^2.$$
Let $\mathcal F:\mathsf{Sh}(\mathbb C^\times,\mathsf{Set})$ be its sheaf of holomorphic sections. Equivalently, this is the sheaf of solutions to the equation \eqref{eq:square-root},
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal F(V)&=\{z:V\to\mathbb C^\times\mid z\text{ holomorphic and }(f\circ z)|^V=\mathrm{id}_V\}\\
&=\{z:V\to\mathbb C^\times\mid z\text{ holomorphic and }z(u)^2=u\text{ for every }u:V\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
As in \cref{par:square-root-monodromy}, on a sufficiently small disc $V$ containing $u_0$, the sections are precisely the two holomorphic solution branches $z_+$ and $z_-$, identifying 
$$\mathcal F|_V\cong\underline{\{+,-\}}_V.$$
Hence $\mathcal F$ is locally constant, with stalk at $u_0$
$$\mathcal F_{u_0}=\{[z_+]_{u_0},[z_-]_{u_0}\}\cong f^{-1}(u_0)$$ 
identified by evaluation. 
However, the sheaf is not constant: $\mathcal F(\mathbb C^\times)=\emptyset$. A global section would determine a germ at $u_0$ fixed by all monodromy at $u_0$, whereas transport once around $0$ exchanges the two square-root germs, as demonstrated in \cref{par:square-root-monodromy}. 
Locally constant (but not necessarily constant) sheaves of sets, such as this, are known as \textit{local systems of sets}. 


\parlabel{par:square-root-transport}For a path $\gamma:[0,1]\to\mathbb C^\times$ from $u_0=\gamma(0)$ to $u_1=\gamma(1)$ and a point $\zeta:f^{-1}(u_0)$ in the fibre, let $\widetilde\gamma_\zeta$ be the unique lift through $f$ satisfying $\widetilde\gamma_\zeta(0)=\zeta$.
We have the \textit{transport map}
\[
T_\gamma:\mathcal F_{u_0}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal F_{u_1},\qquad \zeta\longmapsto\widetilde\gamma_\zeta(1).
\]
with the evaluation identifications $\mathcal F_u\cong f^{-1}(u)$.
Thus we obtain a \textit{transport functor}\footnote{Here $\Pi_1(X)$ denotes the fundamental groupoid of some $X:\mathsf{Top}$, that is, the category whose objects are points of $X$ and whose morphisms are homotopy classes of paths relative to their endpoints, with concatenation as composition.}
\[
T:\Pi_1(\mathbb C^\times)\longrightarrow\mathsf{Set},\qquad u\longmapsto\mathcal F_u,\qquad [\gamma]\longmapsto T_\gamma.
\]
Restricting transport to loops at $u_0$ gives the permutation monodromy representation of $\mathcal F$ at $u_0$, the same permutation monodromy representation as in \cref{par:square-root-monodromy}
$$\rho_{\mathcal F,u_0}:\pi_1^{\mathrm{top}}(\mathbb C^\times,u_0)\rightarrow\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathsf{Set}}(\mathcal F_{u_0}),\qquad [\gamma]\mapsto T_\gamma.$$

In \cref{sec:local-systems}, we will see how to upgrade permutation monodromy representations into linear monodromy representations, which we will just call monodromy representations.
Moreover, one can use this machinery to talk about monodromy in the $\ell$-adic arithmetic context, by replacing $\pi_1^{\mathrm{top}}$ with the étale fundamental group $\pi_1^{\text{ét}}$. 
When $U=\mathrm{Spec}(k)$ for some $k:\mathrm{Field}$, with a choice of separable closure $k^{\mathrm{sep}}$, then $\pi_1(U,u_0)=\mathrm{Gal}(k^{\mathrm{sep}}/k)$, where $u_0=\mathrm{Spec}(k^{\mathrm{sep}})$, is the absolute Galois group of $k$. Hence, a monodromy representation at $u_0$ in this case degenerates into a Galois representation, a ubiquitous arithmetic object. 






\section{Local Systems and Lisse Sheaves}\label{sec:local-systems}


\subsection{Complex Local Systems}\label{subsec:complex-local-systems}



\parlabel{par:hypergeometric-local-system}We have looked at permutation monodromy, but permutation actions do not capture all the situations we wish to study, since a lot of times in nature we encounter actions on vector spaces instead of sets. 
For example, instead of an algebraic equation like the one in \cref{par:square-root-monodromy}, we consider a differential equation, which is known as \textit{Gauss's hypergeometric equation}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gauss-hypergeometric}
u(1-u)\frac{\mathrm d^2 z}{\mathrm d u^2}
+\bigl(c-(a+b+1)u\bigr)\frac{\mathrm d z}{\mathrm d u}
-abz=0
\end{equation}
where $a,b,c:\mathbb C$ are fixed parameters and $z:V\rightarrow\mathbb C$ is an unknown holomorphic function, with $V:\mathsf{Open}(U)$, $U=\widehat{\mathbb C}\setminus\{0,1,\infty\}$, and independent variable $u:V$.
We then define the sheaf of solutions to this equation $\mathcal F:\mathsf{Sh}(U,\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb C})$
\[
\mathcal F(V)=\left\{z:V\rightarrow\mathbb C\;\middle|\;z\text{ holomorphic and satisfies \eqref{eq:gauss-hypergeometric} on }V\right\}.
\]
This is a sheaf of vector spaces since \eqref{eq:gauss-hypergeometric} is a homogeneous linear differential equation. For each $u_0:U$, the existence-and-uniqueness theorem \cite[Theorem~4.3.1]{HottaTakeuchiTanisaki2008} gives a sufficiently small open disc $V:\mathsf{Open}(U)$ containing $u_0$ and an isomorphism of sheaves
\[
\mathcal F|_V\xrightarrow{\sim}\underline{\mathbb C}_V^2
\]
Thus $\mathcal F$ is locally constant of rank $2$. 
We call such a sheaf a \textit{complex local system of rank $2$}.

\begin{definition}[Complex Local System]\label{def:complex-local-system}
Let $U:\mathsf{Man}_{\mathbb C}$ be connected. 
A \textit{complex local system} on $U$ is a locally constant sheaf $\mathcal F:\mathsf{Sh}(U,\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb C})$ with finite-dimensional stalks. 
Explicitly, every $u:U$ has an open neighbourhood $V:\mathsf{Open}(U)$ and an isomorphism
\[
\varphi_V:\mathcal F|_V\xrightarrow{\sim}\underline{\mathbb C}_V^n
\]
called a \textit{local trivialization} at $u$, for some $n:\mathbb N$ called the \textit{rank} of $\mathcal F$. 
Denote by $\mathsf{LocSys}_{\mathbb C}(U)$ the (symmetric monoidal $\mathbb C$-linear) category of complex local systems on $U$. 
\end{definition}
\begin{definition}[Transport]\label{def:complex-transport}
Let $\mathcal F$ be a complex local system of rank $n$ on a connected $U:\mathsf{Man}_{\mathbb C}$. We define the \textit{transport functor} using analytic continuation (see Appendix \cref{par:analytic-continuation})
\[
T:\Pi_1(U)\rightarrow\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb C}^{\mathrm{fin}},\qquad u\mapsto\mathcal F_u,\qquad [\gamma]\mapsto T_\gamma.
\]
where the \textit{transport map} $T_{\gamma}:\mathcal F_{\gamma(0)}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal F_{\gamma(1)}$ for a path $\gamma:[0,1]\to U$ is defined as follows. 
By compactness of closed intervals, we can choose a subdivision $0=t_0<\cdots<t_N=1$ and open sets $V_i:\mathsf{Open}(U)$ containing $\gamma([t_{i-1},t_i])$, with local trivializations
\[
\varphi_i:\mathcal F|_{V_i}\xrightarrow{\sim}\underline{\mathbb C}_{V_i}^n.
\]
Put $v_i=\gamma(t_i)$ for each $i=0,\dots,N$, then the trivializations induce stalk isomorphisms $\varphi_{i,v_i}:\mathcal F_{v_i}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbb C^n$. 
The transport map along $\gamma$ is then defined as the composite
\[
T_\gamma=\bigl(\varphi_{N,v_N}^{-1}\circ\varphi_{N,v_{N-1}}\bigr)\circ\cdots\circ\bigl(\varphi_{1,v_1}^{-1}\circ\varphi_{1,v_0}\bigr):\mathcal F_{\gamma(0)}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal F_{\gamma(1)}.
\]
which is independent of the subdivision and trivializations and depends only on the homotopy class of $\gamma$ relative to its endpoints, by monodromy theorem \cite[Lemma~7.2.7]{Schapira2011AlgebraTopology}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Monodromy]\label{def:monodromy}
Let $\mathcal F$ be a complex local system of rank $n$ on a connected $U:\mathsf{Man}_{\mathbb C}$, and fix a base point $u_0:U$. Restricting its transport functor to loops at $u_0$ gives the \textit{monodromy representation} of $\mathcal F$ at $u_0$
$$\rho_{\mathcal F,u_0}:\pi_1^{\mathrm{top}}(U,u_0)\rightarrow\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb C}}(\mathcal F_{u_0}),\qquad [\gamma]\mapsto T_\gamma,$$
whose image is the \textit{monodromy group} of $\mathcal F$ at $u_0$.
\end{definition}

\parlabel{par:local-system-etale-space}Like in \cref{par:square-root-sheaf}, $\mathcal F$ is also a sheaf of sections of a cover. 
The \textit{espace étalé} of $U$ is
$$\mathrm{\acute{E}t}(U):=\coprod_{u\in U}\mathcal F_u:\mathsf{Man}_{\mathbb C}$$
with topology generated by sets $\widetilde{s}(V)=\{(u,s_u):u:V\}$ for $V:\mathsf{Open}(U)$ and $s:\mathcal F(V)$. 
Each basic open $\widetilde{s}(V)$ is identified with $V$ by $(u,s_u)\mapsto u$, so the complex charts of $U$ pull back to a compatible complex atlas on $\mathrm{\acute{E}t}(U)$.\footnote{Throughout this report, complex manifolds are not required to be second-countable. In particular, the \textit{espace étalé} of a positive-rank complex local system has uncountable discrete fibres and hence is not second-countable.}
Its associated covering map
$$f:\mathrm{\acute{E}t}(U)\rightarrow U,\qquad (u,\xi)\mapsto u$$
is the analogue of the double cover in \cref{par:square-root-sheaf}, and its sheaf of sections recovers $\mathcal F$. Analytic continuation on $U$ is equivalent to path lifting to the espace étalé, so the transport can also be defined equivalently via path lifting, such as in \cref{par:square-root-transport}. 

\parlabel{par:representation-local-system}In the other direction, if we are given a representation 
$$\rho:\pi_1^{\mathrm{top}}(U,u_0)\rightarrow \mathrm{Aut}_{\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb C}}(W)$$
for some connected $U:\mathsf{Man}_{\mathbb C}$, base point $u_0:U$ and $W:\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb C}^{\mathrm{fin}}$ of dimension $n$, we can recover a local system $\mathcal F$ of rank $n$ on $U$ whose monodromy representation is $\rho$ by the following way. 
Pick a universal cover $q:\widetilde{U}\rightarrow U$ and a basepoint lift $\widetilde{u_0}: q^{-1}(u_0)$. 
Define a right $\pi_1^{\mathrm{top}}(U,u_0)$ action on $\widetilde{U}\times W_{\mathrm{disc}}$, where $W_{\mathrm{disc}}$ has the discrete topology, by 
$$(\widetilde{u}, w) \cdot g=(\widetilde{u} \cdot g, \rho(g)^{-1} w) .$$
where $\widetilde{u} \cdot g$ is the action by the deck transformation determined by $\widetilde{u_0}$. Form the orbit space
$$E_{\rho}=(\widetilde{U}\times W_{\mathrm{disc}})/\pi_1^{\mathrm{top}}(U,u_0)$$
We note that $E_{\rho}$ inherits a complex structure induced by its associated covering 
$$f_\rho:E_{\rho}\rightarrow U,\qquad [\widetilde{u},w]\mapsto q(\widetilde{u})$$
for which it is a local biholomorphism i.e. étale cover. These recover the espace étalé of our desired local system and its associated cover. 
Therefore, we can take its sheaf of sections $\mathcal F_\rho$ to obtain our desired local system, where for each $V:\mathsf{Open}(U)$
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal F_\rho(V)
&=\{s:V\to E_\rho\mid s\text{ holomorphic\footnotemark \ and }(f_\rho\circ s)|^V=\mathrm{id}_V\}\\
&\cong\left\{a:q^{-1}(V)\to W_{\mathrm{disc}}\;\middle|\;
\begin{array}{l}
a\text{ locally constant},\\
a(\widetilde u\cdot g)=\rho(g)^{-1}a(\widetilde u)
\end{array}
\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
where the last identification is given by $s(q(\widetilde{u}))=[\widetilde{u},a(\widetilde{u})]$ for every $\widetilde{u}:q^{-1}(V)$. 
\footnotetext{Continuity alone suffices: since $f_\rho$ is a local biholomorphism, every continuous section is locally a holomorphic inverse of $f_\rho$, and hence is holomorphic.}

\begin{definition}[Linear Étale Cover]\label{def:linear-etale-cover}
Let $U:\mathsf{Man}_{\mathbb C}$ be connected.
A \textit{linear étale cover} of $U$ is a complex manifold $E:\mathsf{Man}_{\mathbb C}$ equipped with a holomorphic covering map $f:E\to U$ and a finite-dimensional complex vector-space structure on each fibre, such that every $u:U$ has an open neighbourhood $V:\mathsf{Open}(U)$ and a biholomorphism over $V$
\[
\varphi_V:f^{-1}(V)\xrightarrow{\sim}V\times W_{\mathrm{disc}}
\]
which is linear on each fibre, for some $W:\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb C}^{\mathrm{fin}}$ with $W_{\mathrm{disc}}$ given the discrete topology.
Denote by $\mathsf{\acute{E}t}^{\mathrm{lin}}_{\mathbb C}(U)$ the category of these covers, with fibrewise linear holomorphic maps over $U$ as morphisms.
\end{definition}

\parlabel{par:complex-monodromy-equivalence}For each $U:\mathsf{Man}_{\mathbb C}$ and $u_0:U$, there are equivalences of categories \cite[\S~7.2]{MacPherson1990}
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf{\acute{E}t}^{\mathrm{lin}}_{\mathbb C}(U)
\simeq\mathsf{LocSys}_{\mathbb C}(U)
\simeq^{\otimes}\mathsf{Rep}_{\mathbb C}^{\mathrm{fin}}\bigl(\pi_1^{\mathrm{top}}(U,u_0)\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]
Moreover, the last equivalence is of symmetric monoidal $\mathbb C$-linear categories.


\begin{example}\label{ex:complex-smooth-proper-families}A rich source of local systems is smooth and proper families. Let
$$f:E\rightarrow U$$
be a smooth proper map between complex manifolds. The fibres $E_{u_0}:=f^{-1}(u_0)$ at $u_0:U$ are compact complex manifolds. 
The higher direct image sheaves
$$\mathbf R^q f_{*}\underline{\mathbb C}$$
are complex local systems for each $q:\mathbb N$ by Ehresmann's fibration theorem \cite[Theorem~9.3]{Voisin2002HodgeI}.
Explicitly, at $u_0:U$, there exists open neighborhood $V:\mathsf{Open}(U)$ of $u_0$ with 
$$\mathbf R^qf_*\underline{\mathbb C}|_V\cong \underline{\mathrm H^q(E_{u_0},\underline{\mathbb C})}_V$$
In particular on the level of stalks 
$\bigl(\mathbf R^qf_*\underline{\mathbb C}\bigr)_{u_0}\cong\mathrm H^q(E_{u_0},\underline{\mathbb C})$. 
\end{example}

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{$\ell$}{l}-adic Local Systems}\label{subsec:ell-adic-local-systems}

We now develop the $\ell$-adic analogues of the constructions in \cref{subsec:complex-local-systems}.

In number theory, $\ell$-adic Galois representations are ubiquitous and encode a lot of intricate arithmetic information. 
Let $E:\mathsf{Curv}_k^{\mathrm{sm}}$ be an elliptic curve over a field $k:\mathsf{Field}$, recall that its Tate module at a prime $\ell:\mathbb N$ is the $\mathbb Z_\ell[\Gamma_k]$-module 
$$\mathrm{T}_\ell(E)=\lim_{\longleftarrow}E[\ell^n]$$
where $\Gamma_k=\mathrm{Gal}(k^{\mathrm{sep}}/k)$. In other words, it is an (continuous) $\ell$-adic Galois representation
$$\rho_{E,\ell}:\Gamma_k\rightarrow\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathbb Z_\ell}(\mathrm{T}_\ell (E))$$
Theorems of Faltings and Zarhin–Mori show that the rationalized Tate module
$$\mathrm{V}_\ell(E):=\mathrm{T}_\ell(E)\otimes_{\mathbb Z_\ell} \mathbb Q_\ell$$
as a $\mathbb Q_\ell [\Gamma_k]$-module determines the elliptic curve up to $k$-isogeny, assuming $k$ is finitely generated over its prime field \cite{Faltings1983,Zarhin1976,Mori1978}. 
In addition, the Tate module also contains information about reduction types (c.f. Néron–Ogg–Shafarevich criterion \cite[Theorem~VII.7.1]{Silverman2009} and Serre–Tate theorem \cite[Theorem~1]{SerreTate1968}), endomorphisms (c.f. Tate's theorem \cite[Main Theorem]{Tate1966}), point counts over finite fields (c.f. Grothendieck–Lefschetz trace formula \cite[Theorem~29.4]{Milne2013EtaleCohomology}), and many more. 


\begin{notation}We use the following notation:
\begin{notationtable}
  k & \mathsf{Field} & A field \\
  U & \mathsf{Sch}_{\operatorname{Spec}(k)} & A Noetherian scheme over \(k\) \\
  \ell & \mathbb N & A prime with \(\ell\ne\operatorname{char}(k)\) \\
  \Lambda & \mathsf{Ring}^{\mathrm{top}} & One of \(\mathbb Z_\ell\), \(\mathbb Q_\ell\), or \(\mathbb Z/\ell^n\mathbb Z\) for \(n:\mathbb N\) \\
  U_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}} & \mathsf{Site} & The small étale site of \(U\)
\end{notationtable}
\end{notation}

\begin{definition}[Mod-$\ell^n$ Sheaf]
  For each $n:\mathbb N$, a \textit{mod-$\ell^n$ sheaf} on $U$ is a sheaf of $\mathbb Z/\ell^n\mathbb Z$-modules on $U_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}$. Denote their (symmetric monoidal $\mathbb Z/\ell^n\mathbb Z$-linear) category as
  \[
  \mathsf{Sh}(U,\mathbb Z/\ell^n\mathbb Z)
  :=\mathsf{Sh}(U_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}},\mathsf{Mod}_{\mathbb Z/\ell^n\mathbb Z}).
  \]
  A mod-$\ell^n$ sheaf is \textit{lisse} if it is locally constant with finite geometric stalks. We denote by $\mathsf{LocSys}_{\mathbb Z/\ell^n\mathbb Z}(U)$ the (symmetric monoidal $\mathbb Z/\ell^n\mathbb Z$-linear) full subcategory of lisse sheaves. 
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[$\ell$-adic Sheaf]
  An \textit{(integral) $\ell$-adic sheaf} on $U$ is a family $\mathcal F$ of sheaves $$\mathcal F_n:\mathsf{Sh}(U,\mathbb Z/\ell^n\mathbb Z)$$ equipped with maps $r_n:\mathcal F_{n+1}\rightarrow \mathcal F_n$ in $\mathsf{Sh}(U,\mathbb Z/\ell^{n+1}\mathbb Z)$, such that 
  $$\overline{r_n}:\mathcal F_{n+1}/\ell^n\mathcal F_{n+1}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal F_n$$
  factoring through $r_n$ is an isomorphism in $\mathsf{Sh}(U,\mathbb Z/\ell^{n+1}\mathbb Z)$, for each $n:\mathbb N$. 
  Moreover, we say that $\mathcal F$ is \textit{lisse} if each $\mathcal F_n$ is lisse. 
  Denote by $\mathsf{Sh}(U,\mathbb Z_\ell)$ the (symmetric monoidal $\mathbb Z_\ell$-linear) category of $\ell$-adic sheaves on $U$, and $\mathsf{LocSys}_{\mathbb Z_\ell}(U)$ the (symmetric monoidal $\mathbb Z_\ell$-linear) full subcategory of lisse sheaves. 
\end{definition}
\begin{definition}[Rational $\ell$-adic Sheaf]
  Define the category of \textit{rational $\ell$-adic sheaves} on $U$, denoted $\mathsf{Sh}(U,\mathbb Q_\ell)$, as the (symmetric monoidal $\mathbb Q_\ell$-linear) category where the objects are the same as $\mathsf{Sh}(U,\mathbb Z_\ell)$ but the morphisms between two objects $\mathcal F,\mathcal G:\mathsf{Sh}(U,\mathbb Q_\ell)$ are 
  $$\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathsf{Sh}(U,\mathbb Q_\ell)}(\mathcal F,\mathcal G):=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathsf{Sh}(U,\mathbb Z_\ell)}(\mathcal F,\mathcal G)\otimes_{\mathbb Z_\ell}\mathbb Q_\ell$$
  considered as tensoring of $\mathbb Z_\ell$-modules. 
  An object of this category is a rational $\ell$-adic sheaf on $U$. There is a canonical functor called \textit{rationalization}
  $$(-)_{\mathbb Q_\ell}=(-)\otimes_{\mathbb Z_\ell}\mathbb Q_\ell:\mathsf{Sh}(U,\mathbb Z_\ell)\rightarrow \mathsf{Sh}(U,\mathbb Q_\ell),\qquad \mathcal F\mapsto\mathcal F,\qquad f\mapsto f\otimes 1.$$
  A rational $\ell$-adic sheaf $\mathcal F$ on $U$ is \textit{lisse} if there exists a lisse integral $\ell$-adic sheaf $\mathcal L$ and an isomorphism
  $\mathcal F\cong\mathcal L\otimes_{\mathbb Z_\ell}\mathbb Q_\ell.$ 
  Denote by $\mathsf{LocSys}_{\mathbb Q_\ell}(U)$ the (symmetric monoidal $\mathbb Q_\ell$-linear) full subcategory of lisse sheaves of $\mathsf{Sh}(U,\mathbb Q_\ell)$. 
\end{definition}

\parlabel{para:clunkiness}Many common sheaf constructions for integral $\ell$-adic sheaves can be taken level-wise such as pullbacks, direct sums, and tensor products, but unfortunately there are also many common sheaf constructions that require corrections, since an operation could produce a family $\mathcal A$ that does not satisfy 
$$\mathcal A_{n+1}/\ell^n \mathcal A_{n+1}\cong \mathcal A_n$$
Whenever such a representative exists, we replace the family with an object that does satisfy this condition and is isomorphic to it in the Artin–Rees category \cite[Exp.~V]{SGA5}. 
Explicitly, we replace $\mathcal A$ with $\mathcal A^\prime$ where 
$$\mathcal A^\prime_n=\mathcal B_N/\ell^n\mathcal B_N,\qquad\text{where}\qquad \mathcal B_n:=\bigcap_{m\ge n}\mathrm{Im}(\mathcal A_m\longrightarrow\mathcal A_n),$$
for each $n:\mathbb N$, with $N:\mathbb N$ sufficiently large, dependent on $n$.
Constructions that require such corrections include kernels, images, sheaf Hom, and duals.

We note that Bhatt and Scholze \cite{BhattScholze2015} have developed the framework of \textit{pro-étale sites}, allowing $\ell$-adic sheaves to be considered as bona fide sheaves on the pro-étale site $U_{\mathrm{pro\text{-}\acute{e}t}}$, which removes the clunkiness we had to deal with in \cref{para:clunkiness}, \cref{para:clunkiness2}, and \cref{para:clunkiness3}. 

Recall the étale fundamental group of a connected $U$ at a geometric base point $u_0:\mathrm{Spec}(\Omega)\rightarrow U$ is the profinite group defined by the inverse limit
$$\pi_1^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(U,u_0):=\lim_{\substack{\longleftarrow\\(X,x_0)\to(U,u_0)}}\mathrm{Aut}_U(X):\mathsf{Grp}^{\mathrm{pro}}$$
where the limit is taken over connected finite étale Galois covers $X\rightarrow U$ with $x_0$ a lift of $u_0$, and the transition map $\varphi:\mathrm{Aut}_U(Y)\rightarrow\mathrm{Aut}_U(X) $ for a pointed map $h:(Y,y_0)\rightarrow (X,x_0)$ between conneced finite étale Galois covers is given by $h\circ \sigma=\varphi(\sigma)\circ h$, for $\sigma:\mathrm{Aut}_U(Y)$. 

Alternatively, one can construct the étale fundamental group via the \textit{fibre functor}
$$\omega_{u_0}:\mathsf{\acute{E}t}^{\mathrm{fin}}(U)\rightarrow\mathsf{Set}^{\mathrm{fin}},\qquad E\mapsto \mathrm{Hom}_U(\mathrm{Spec}(\Omega),E)$$
where $\mathsf{\acute{E}t}^{\mathrm{fin}}(U)$ is the category of finite étale covers $X\rightarrow U$ with morphisms over $U$. 
We can then construct the étale fundamental group as 
$$\pi_1^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(U,u_0)=\mathrm{Aut}(\omega_{u_0}):\mathsf{Grp}^{\mathrm{pro}}$$
with topology given by the subspace topology induced by
$$\pi_1^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(U,u_0)\hookrightarrow \prod_{E\,:\,\mathsf{\acute{E}t}^{\mathrm{fin}}(U)}\mathrm{Sym}(\omega_{u_0}(E))$$
where each symmetric group is given the discrete topology. 

Just as in \cref{par:complex-monodromy-equivalence}, for connected $U$ and a geometric base point $u_0:\operatorname{Spec}(\Omega)\to U$, we have a symmetric monoidal $\Lambda$-linear equivalence of categories
\[
\mathsf{LocSys}_\Lambda(U)
\simeq^{\otimes}
\mathsf{Rep}_\Lambda^{\mathrm{fin}}\bigl(\pi_1^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(U,u_0)\bigr)
\]
If $\Lambda$ is $\mathbb Q_\ell$ or $\mathbb F_\ell$, the \textit{algebraic monodromy group} of a lisse sheaf $\mathcal F$ at $u_0$ is the Zariski closure of the image of its associated representation in the algebraic group $\operatorname{GL}(\mathcal F_{u_0})$.

Let $\Lambda=\mathbb Z/\ell^n\mathbb Z$ for $n:\mathbb N$, and $\mathcal F:\mathsf{LocSys}_{\Lambda}(U)$. Let $E\rightarrow U$ be the finite étale cover representing the underlying sheaf of sets of $\mathcal F$, we have a (continuous) representation
$$\rho_{\mathcal F}:\pi_1^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(U,u_0)\rightarrow \mathrm{Aut}_{\mathsf{Mod}_{\Lambda}}(\mathcal F_{u_0})$$
with the action by $\sigma:\pi_1^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(U,u_0)$ induced by 
$$\sigma_E:\omega_{u_0}(E)\rightarrow \omega_{u_0}(E)$$
under the canonical identification 
$$\mathcal F_{u_0}=\varinjlim_{\substack{q\,:\,V\to U\ \text{étale}\\v\,:\,\operatorname{Spec}(\Omega)\to V\ \text{geo. pt.}\\q\,\circ \,v\,=\,u_0}}\mathcal F(V)\xlongrightarrow{\sim}\omega_{u_0}(E),\qquad [(V,v),s]\mapsto s\circ v$$
where $(s:V\rightarrow E):\mathrm{Hom}_U(V,E)=\mathcal F(V)$ is representing a section. 

\parlabel{par:etale-representation-local-system}Conversely, given a (continuous) representation in $\mathsf{Rep}^{\mathrm{fin}}_{\Lambda}(\pi_1^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(U,u_0))$
$$\rho:\pi_1^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(U,u_0)\rightarrow\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathsf{Mod}_{\Lambda}}(M)$$
Let $N=\mathrm{Ker}(\rho)$, which is open by continuity, so $G=\pi_1^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(U,u_0)/N$ is finite and $\rho$ factorizes
$$\pi_1^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(U,u_0)\twoheadrightarrow G\xlongrightarrow{\overline{\rho}}\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathsf{Mod}_{\Lambda}}(M).$$
By Grothendieck's Galois theory (see \cref{app:grothendieck-galois-theory}), the $\pi_1^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(U,u_0)$-set $G$ corresponds to a finite étale cover $f:Y\rightarrow U$ with $\omega_{u_0}(Y)\cong G$ as $\pi_1^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(U,u_0)$-sets. 
By the identification we have a free transitive right $G$-action on $\omega_{u_0}(Y)$ equivariant with the $\pi_1^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(U,u_0)$-action. 
By Grothendieck's Galois theory, this corresponds to a deck transformation of $Y$ transitive and free on each geometric fibre, which makes $Y$ an étale $G$-torsor. 
By finiteness of $M$, set $M_U:=(\coprod_{m\,:\, M}U\rightarrow U):\mathsf{Sch}_U$ and form the space called the \textit{contracted product}
$$E_{\rho}:=Y\times^G M:=\left.\left(Y\times_U M_U\right)\middle/G\right.\cong\left.\left(\coprod_{m\,:\, M}Y\right)\middle/G\right.$$ 
where $G$ acts by $(y,m)\cdot g=(yg,\overline{\rho}(g)^{-1}m)$. 
There is a unique map $p:E_{\rho}\rightarrow U$ factoring through the $G$-invariant composite map
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=large, row sep=1.8em]
  & E_\rho \arrow[dr, "p", bend left=15] & \\
  Y\times_U M_U \arrow[ur, two heads, "q", bend left=15]
    \arrow[r, "\mathrm{pr}_Y"'] & Y \arrow[r, "f"'] & U
\end{tikzcd}
\]
where $q$ is the quotient map. Thus we found a finite étale cover 
$$(E_\rho\xlongrightarrow{p}U):\mathsf{\acute{E}t}^{\mathrm{fin}}(U)$$
Moreover, the module operations on $M$ are $G$-equivariant and descend to addition, zero, and scalar-multiplication morphisms over $U$, making $E_\rho$ a $\Lambda$-module scheme over $U$.
By taking its sheaf of sections 
$$\mathcal F_{\rho}(V):=\mathrm{Hom}_U(V,E_{\rho})=\{s:V\rightarrow E_{\rho}\mid p\circ s=q\}:\mathsf{Mod}_{\Lambda},$$
for $q:V\rightarrow U$ étale, we obtain our desired lisse sheaf $\mathcal F_\rho:\mathsf{LocSys}_{\Lambda}(U)$. 
To see that $\mathcal F_\rho$ is étale-locally constant, note that pulling back by $f:Y\rightarrow U$ gives
$$\mathcal F_\rho|_Y\cong f^*\mathcal F_\rho\cong\mathrm{Hom}_Y(-,Y\times_U E_\rho)\cong\mathrm{Hom}_Y\left(-,\coprod_{m\,:\,M}Y\right)\cong\underline{M}_Y.$$

\parlabel{para:Zell}Let $\Lambda=\mathbb Z_\ell$. Given a continuous representation 
$$\rho:\pi_1^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(U,u_0)\rightarrow\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathbb Z_\ell}(M)$$ on a finitely generated $\mathbb Z_\ell$-module $M:\mathsf{Mod}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{top}}$ with its $\ell$-adic topology, apply \cref{par:etale-representation-local-system} to the induced representations $\rho_n$ on $M_n:=M/\ell^nM$ for $n\ge1$, obtaining sheaves $\mathcal F_n$. 
The reductions $M_{n+1}\twoheadrightarrow M_n$ are equivariant, and, by Grothendieck's Galois theory, they induce transition maps $r_n:\mathcal F_{n+1}\rightarrow\mathcal F_n$ satisfying
$$\mathcal F_{n+1}/\ell^n\mathcal F_{n+1}\cong\mathcal F_n,$$
Thus we obtain the integral lisse $\ell$-adic sheaf $\mathcal F$. 
Conversely, for a lisse sheaf $\mathcal F$, the stalk $\mathcal F_{u_0}$ has a natural $\pi_1^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(U,u_0)$-action by taking the $\pi_1^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(U,u_0)$-action with the induce map on each level, and assembling them together. 

\parlabel{para:Qell}Let $\Lambda=\mathbb Q_\ell$. Given a continuous representation $\rho:\pi_1^{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(U,u_0)\rightarrow\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathbb Q_\ell}(M)$ on a finite-dimensional $\mathbb Q_\ell$-vector space $M$, choose a $\rho$-stable $\mathbb Z_\ell$-lattice $L\subset M$, which exists because the image of $\rho$ is compact \cite[Part~II, Ch.~IV, Appendix~1, Theorem~1]{Serre1992Lie}. Apply \cref{para:Zell} to the action on $L$, obtaining an integral lisse sheaf $\mathcal F_L$, and set $\mathcal F_\rho:=\mathcal F_L\otimes_{\mathbb Z_\ell}\mathbb Q_\ell$.
Conversely, choose an integral lisse model $\mathcal G$ of a rational lisse sheaf $\mathcal F\cong\mathcal G\otimes_{\mathbb Z_\ell}\mathbb Q_\ell$. Extend the stalk action from \cref{para:Zell} by scalars:
$$\mathcal F_{u_0}=\mathcal G_{u_0}\otimes_{\mathbb Z_\ell}\mathbb Q_\ell,\qquad \rho_{\mathcal F}(\gamma):=\rho_{\mathcal G}(\gamma)\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb Q_\ell}.$$


\begin{example}[Local Systems from Torsors]\label{ex:torsor}
  Given a finite group $G:\mathsf{Grp}^{\mathrm{fin}}$, a finitely generated $\Lambda$-module $M:\mathsf{Mod}^{\mathrm{top}}_{\Lambda}$, a continuous representation $\rho:G\rightarrow\mathrm{Aut}_\Lambda(M)$ with $G$ given the discrete topology, and a finite étale right $G$-torsor $f:Y\rightarrow U$, we construct a local system $\mathcal L_\rho:\mathsf{LocSys}_\Lambda(U)$ by following the same procedure in \cref{par:etale-representation-local-system}, \cref{para:Zell}, and \cref{para:Qell}. 
  In other words, the following. 
  \begin{enumerate}
  \item For $\Lambda=\mathbb Z/\ell^n\mathbb Z$, as in \cref{par:etale-representation-local-system}, form the cover 
  $$E_\rho:=Y\times^G M=\left.\left(Y\times_U M_U\right)\middle/G\right.\xlongrightarrow{p}U,$$
  where $M_U:=(\coprod_{m\,:\,M}U\rightarrow U):\mathsf{Sch}_U$ and $G$ acts by $(y,m)\cdot g=(yg,\rho(g)^{-1}m)$, then take the sheaf of sections to obtain the lisse sheaf
  $$\mathcal L_\rho:=\mathrm{Hom}_U(-,E_\rho):\mathsf{LocSys}_\Lambda(U).$$
  
  \item For $\Lambda=\mathbb Z_\ell$, as in \cref{para:Zell}, apply the finite-coefficient construction to $M_n:=M/\ell^nM$ with its induced $G$-action to obtain $\mathcal L_n$. The $G$-equivariant reductions $M_{n+1}\twoheadrightarrow M_n$ induce maps of associated covers and hence transition maps $r_n:\mathcal L_{n+1}\rightarrow\mathcal L_n$ by composition of sections. These satisfy $\mathcal L_{n+1}/\ell^n\mathcal L_{n+1}\cong\mathcal L_n$, giving
  $\mathcal L_\rho:\mathsf{LocSys}_{\mathbb Z_\ell}(U).$

  \item For $\Lambda=\mathbb Q_\ell$, as in \cref{para:Qell}, choose a $G$-stable $\mathbb Z_\ell$-lattice $L\subset M$ and apply the integral construction $L$ to obtain $\mathcal L_L$, then rationalize:
  $\mathcal L_\rho:=\mathcal L_L\otimes_{\mathbb Z_\ell}\mathbb Q_\ell:\mathsf{LocSys}_{\mathbb Q_\ell}(U).$
  \end{enumerate}
\end{example}

\begin{example}We give some concrete examples of \cref{ex:torsor}. 
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item Let $N:\mathbb N$ be invertible in $k$, and assume that $k$ contains all $N$-th roots of unity. 
    Let $Y=U=\mathbb G_m/k$, $G=\mu_N(k)$, and the $G$-torsor $f:Y\rightarrow U$ by $z\mapsto z^N$ with the right multiplication action $z\cdot\zeta=z\zeta$ for $\zeta:\mu_N(k)$. 
    Let $K/\mathbb Q_\ell$ be a finite field extension, and let $M=K$ if $\Lambda=\mathbb Q_\ell$, $M=\mathcal O_K$ if $\Lambda=\mathbb Z_\ell$, and $M=\mathcal O_K/\ell^n\mathcal O_K$ if $\Lambda=\mathbb Z/\ell^n\mathbb Z$. 
    Choose a character $\chi:\mu_N(k)\rightarrow M^\times$; then we get the representation
    $$\rho_\chi:G\rightarrow\mathrm{Aut}_\Lambda(M),\qquad \rho_\chi(\zeta)(m):=\chi(\zeta)m.$$
    The lisse sheaf we obtain from these data, denoted $\mathcal L_\chi$, is the \textit{Kummer sheaf} of $\chi$. 
    \item Assume positive characteristic $\operatorname{char}(k)=p\ne \ell$. Let $Y=U=\mathbb G_a/k$, $G=\mathbb F_p$, and let $f:Y\rightarrow U$ be the $G$-torsor $z\mapsto z^p-z$ under the translation action $z\cdot a=z+a$.
    Let $K/\mathbb Q_\ell$ be a finite field extension containing a primitive $p$-th root of unity, and let $M=K$ if $\Lambda=\mathbb Q_\ell$, $M=\mathcal O_K$ if $\Lambda=\mathbb Z_\ell$, and $M=\mathcal O_K/\ell^n\mathcal O_K$ if $\Lambda=\mathbb Z/\ell^n\mathbb Z$.
    Choose a (additive) character $\psi:\mathbb F_p\rightarrow M^\times$; then we get the representation
    $$\rho_\psi:G\rightarrow\mathrm{Aut}_\Lambda(M),\qquad \rho_\psi(a)(m):=\psi(a)m.$$
    The lisse sheaf we obtain from these data, denoted $\mathcal L_\psi$, is the \textit{Artin--Schreier sheaf} of $\psi$. 
    \item We can generalize the above. 
    Let $k=\mathbb F_q$ and $H/k$ a smooth connected commutative algebraic group with $q$-power Frobenius $F:H\rightarrow H$. 
    Let $Y=U=H$ and $G=H(\mathbb F_q)$. By Lang's theorem, there is a $G$-torsor 
    $$f:H\rightarrow H,\qquad h\mapsto F(h)h^{-1}$$
    with action given by right multiplication $h\cdot g=hg$ for $g:G$.
    Let $K/\mathbb Q_\ell$ be a finite field extension, and let $M=K$ if $\Lambda=\mathbb Q_\ell$, $M=\mathcal O_K$ if $\Lambda=\mathbb Z_\ell$, and $M=\mathcal O_K/\ell^n\mathcal O_K$ if $\Lambda=\mathbb Z/\ell^n\mathbb Z$.
    Choose a character $\chi:G\rightarrow M^\times$; then we get the representation
    $$\rho_\chi:G\rightarrow\mathrm{Aut}_\Lambda(M),\qquad \rho_\chi(g)(m):=\chi(g)m.$$
    The lisse sheaf we obtain from these data, denoted $\mathcal L_\chi$, is the \textit{Lang sheaf} of $\chi$.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Tate Twists]
  Define the lisse sheaf $\Lambda(1):\mathsf{LocSys}_{\Lambda}(U)$ as
$$\Lambda(1):=
\begin{cases}
  \mu_{\ell^n}, & \Lambda=\mathbb Z/\ell^n\mathbb Z,\\[4pt]
  ((\mathbb Z/\ell^n\mathbb Z)(1))_{j\,:\,\mathbb N}, & \Lambda=\mathbb Z_\ell,\\[4pt]
  \mathbb Z_\ell(1)\otimes_{\mathbb Z_\ell}\mathbb Q_\ell, & \Lambda=\mathbb Q_\ell,
\end{cases}$$
For $m:\mathbb Z$, define the lisse sheaf $\Lambda(m):\mathsf{LocSys}_{\Lambda}(U)$ as
$$\Lambda(m):=
\begin{cases}
  \Lambda(1)^{\otimes m}, & m>0,\\[4pt]
  \underline{\Lambda}, & m=0,\\[4pt]
  \bigl(\Lambda(1)^{\otimes(-m)}\bigr)^\vee, & m<0,
\end{cases}$$
where $(-)^\vee$ denotes the $\Lambda$-linear dual. The \textit{Tate twist} of an $\ell$-adic sheaf $\mathcal F:\mathsf{Sh}(U,\Lambda)$ is 
$$\mathcal F(m):=\mathcal F\otimes_{\Lambda} \Lambda(m)$$
where $m:\mathbb N$, which is lisse iff $\mathcal F$ is lisse. 
\end{example}

\begin{example}\label{ex:etale-smooth-proper-families}A rich source of lisse sheaves is smooth proper families, as in \cref{ex:complex-smooth-proper-families}. Let
$$f:E\rightarrow U$$
be a smooth proper morphism of $k$-schemes. The geometric fibres $E_{u_0}:=E\times_U\operatorname{Spec}\Omega$ at geometric points $u_0:\operatorname{Spec}\Omega\rightarrow U$ are smooth proper $\Omega$-schemes.
The higher direct image 
$$\mathbf R^q f_*\underline{\Lambda}_E$$
are lisse on $U$ for each $q:\mathbb N$ by smooth proper base change \cite[Theorem~20.2 and \S19]{Milne2013EtaleCohomology}, i.e. for $\Lambda=\mathbb Z/\ell^n\mathbb Z$, every geometric point $u_0$ has an \'{e}tale neighbourhood $(V,v_0)$ with
$$\left.(\mathbf R^q f_*\underline{\Lambda}_E)\right|_V\cong\underline{\mathrm H^q_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(E_{u_0},\underline{\Lambda})}_V.$$
Proper base change identifies the geometric stalks as
$$\bigl(\mathbf R^q f_*\underline{\Lambda}_E\bigr)_{u_0}\cong\mathrm H^q_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(E_{u_0},\underline{\Lambda}).$$

\end{example}

\subsection{Tannakian Formalism}

Tannaka showed that compact groups can be recovered from their finite-dimensional unitary representations \cite{Tannaka1939}. Grothendieck later introduced the Tannakian formalism \cite[\S1]{Milne2025TannakianOrigins}, which provides a general framework for what was discussed in \cref{subsec:complex-local-systems} and \cref{subsec:ell-adic-local-systems}.

\begin{definition}[Neutral Tannakian Category]A \textit{neutral tannakian category} $\mathsf{C}$ over $\Lambda:\mathsf{Field}$ is a rigid symmetric monoidal abelian $\Lambda$-linear category, equipped with an exact faithful $\Lambda$-linear symmetric monoidal functor called the \textit{fibre functor}
  $$\omega:\mathsf{C}\rightarrow \mathsf{Vect}_\Lambda^{\mathrm{fin}}$$
  and is such that the scalar map $\Lambda\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{End}(\mathbf 1)$ is an isomorphism. 
\end{definition}

Let $\Lambda:\mathsf{Field}$ be one of $\mathbb C$, $\mathbb Q_\ell$, or $\mathbb F_\ell$  with $\ell\ne \mathrm{char}(k)$ for $k:\mathsf{Field}$, then
the category $\mathsf{LocSys}_{\Lambda}(U)$ of local systems or lisse sheaves on $U:\mathsf{Man}_{\mathbb C}$ or $U:\mathsf{Var}_{k}$ is a neutral tannakian category. 
Its fibre functor is 
$$\omega_{u_0}:\mathsf{LocSys}_{\Lambda}(U)\rightarrow \mathsf{Vect}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{fin}},\qquad \mathcal F\mapsto \mathcal F_{u_0}$$
at a base point $u_0:U$ or geometric point $u_0:\mathrm{Spec}(\Omega)\rightarrow U$. 


\begin{definition}Let $\mathsf C$ be a neutral Tannakian category over $\Lambda:\mathsf{Field}$ with fibre functor $\omega:\mathsf{C}\rightarrow \mathsf{Vect}_\Lambda^{\mathrm{fin}}$, then its \textit{Tannakian group} is the functor $G:\mathsf{Alg}_\Lambda\rightarrow\mathsf{Grp}$
  $$G(R)=\mathrm{Aut}^{\otimes}(\omega_R),\qquad \omega_R(\mathcal F):=\omega(\mathcal F)\otimes_\Lambda R,\qquad {\mathcal F:\mathsf C}$$
  where $\mathrm{Aut}^\otimes$ denotes the natural automorphisms compatible with tensor products and the tensor unit. 
  Moreover, the \textit{monodromy group} of $\mathcal F:\mathsf C$ is the functor $G_\mathcal{F}:\mathsf{Alg}_\Lambda\rightarrow\mathsf{Grp}$
  $$G_{\mathcal F}(R)=\mathrm{Aut}^\otimes(\omega_R\mid_{\langle\mathcal F\rangle_\otimes})$$
  where $\langle\mathcal F\rangle_\otimes$ is the strictly full subcategory of $\mathsf C$ consisting of subquotients of finite direct sums of tensor products of $\mathcal F$ and $\mathcal F^\vee$, including the empty tensor product $\mathbf1$. 
  We remark that this subcategory is also neutral Tannakian over the same field. 
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Tannakian Reconstruction Theorem]
  Let $\mathsf C$ be a neutral Tannakian category over $\Lambda:\mathsf{Field}$ with fibre functor $\omega:\mathsf{C}\rightarrow \mathsf{Vect}_\Lambda^{\mathrm{fin}}$, then its Tannakian group $G:\mathsf{Alg}_\Lambda\rightarrow\mathsf{Grp}$ is representable as an affine group scheme. Moreover $\omega$ induces a $\Lambda$-linear symmetric monoidal equivalence of categories
  $$\mathsf{C}\simeq^{\otimes}\mathsf{Rep}^{\mathrm{alg},\mathrm{fin}}_\Lambda(G)$$
  where the right hand side is the category of algebraic representations of an affine group scheme. 
\end{theorem}

We remark that this formalism is useful in helping to define monodromy groups in the context of the $p$-adic analogue of lisse $\ell$-adic sheaves, where monodromy representations might be unavailable; see \cite[\S2]{Crew1992Isocrystals}.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:surface-fundamental-group}
Let $X:\mathsf{Riem}$ be compact of genus $g:\mathbb N$, let $S=\{s_1,\dots,s_m\}$ be a set of $m$ distinct points of $X$, and put $U=X\setminus S$. For a base point $u_0:U$, we have
\[
\pi_1^{\mathrm{top}}(U,u_0)\cong
\left\langle a_1,b_1,\dots,a_g,b_g,\gamma_1,\dots,\gamma_m
\;\middle|\;
\prod_{i=1}^{g}[a_i,b_i]\,\gamma_1\cdots\gamma_m=1
\right\rangle,
\]
where $[a,b]:=aba^{-1}b^{-1}$, and the generators are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item the loops running around the $i$-th handle $a_1,\dots,a_g$;
  \item the loops passing through the $i$-th handle $b_1,\dots,b_g$;
  \item the loops $\gamma_1,\dots,\gamma_m$ encircling the puncture $s_1,\dots,s_m$ once counterclockwise.
\end{enumerate}
