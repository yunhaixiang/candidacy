\subsection{Motivations}\label{subsec:motivations}

\textit{Monodromy groups} are interesting mathematical objects that arise naturally in both geometric and arithmetic contexts. 
Roughly speaking, they describe how locally defined data change when transported along a loop. 
Controlling and understanding monodromy groups has been a central theme in both of these worlds, with significant applications.
We will discuss both the geometric and arithmetic sides, but this write-up will primarily focus on the arithmetic side, with the geometric side providing motivation and intuition.

\parlabel{par:square-root-monodromy}Let parameter $w:\mathbb C^\times$, and consider the equation
$$z^2=w$$
for the unknown $z:\mathbb C^\times$. Fix a base point $w_0:\mathbb C^\times$ and choose $z_0:\mathbb C^\times$ with $z_0^2=w_0$. 
Near $w_0$, the equation has two holomorphic solution branches $z_+=\sqrt{w}$ and $z_-=-\sqrt{w}$, uniquely specified by $z_+(w_0)=z_0$ and $z_-(w_0)=-z_0$. Suppose we move $w$ once around $0$ along the loop based at $w_0$ given by 
$$w(t)=w_0e^{2\pi i t}\quad t:[0,1]$$
Then we have $z_{+}(t)=z_0e^{\pi i t}$ and $z_{-}(t)=-z_0e^{\pi i t}$. As $t$ goes from $0$ to $1$, the two solution branches swap rather than return to themselves.
In other words, there is a permutation representation of the fundamental group\footnote{with the discrete topology} $\pi^{\mathrm{top}}_1(\mathbb C^\times,w_0)\cong\mathbb Z:\mathsf{Grp}^{\mathrm{top}}$
$$\rho:\pi^{\mathrm{top}}_1(\mathbb C^\times,w_0)\rightarrow\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathsf{Set}}(\{z_+,z_-\})\quad n\mapsto (z_{+}\ z_{-})^n$$
We call this representation the \textit{permutation monodromy representation} and its image, $\mathrm{S}_2$, the \textit{permutation monodromy group} of the equation.

Let us reformulate the above in terms of sheaves. Consider the covering
$$f:\mathbb C^\times\rightarrow\mathbb C^\times\quad z\mapsto z^2$$
Let $\mathcal F:\mathsf{Sh}(\mathbb C^\times,\mathsf{Set})$ be the sheaf of sections of this cover. Thus, for $U:\mathrm{Open}(\mathbb C^\times)$,
$$\begin{aligned}\mathcal F(U)&=\{z:U\to\mathbb C^\times:(f\circ z)|^{U}=\mathrm{id}_U\}\\
&=\{z:U\to\mathbb C^\times:z(w)^2=w,\,\text{for }w:U\}
\end{aligned}$$
This is a locally constant sheaf: for each $w_0 :\mathbb C^\times$, one can find an open neighborhood $U$ of $w_0$ on which the sections are precisely the two square-root branches
$$\begin{aligned}
z_+&:U\rightarrow\mathbb C^\times\quad w\mapsto \sqrt{w}\\
z_-&:U\rightarrow\mathbb C^\times\quad w\mapsto -\sqrt{w}\\
\end{aligned}$$ 
Hence the stalk $\mathcal F_{w_0}=\{z_+,z_-\}\cong f^{-1}(w_0)$ by evaluation, and therefore the aforementioned permutation representation can be rewritten as 
$$\rho:\pi_1^{\mathrm{top}}(\mathbb C^\times,w_0)\rightarrow \mathrm{Aut}_{\mathsf{Set}}(\mathcal F_{w_0})\quad \gamma\mapsto [z\mapsto\widetilde{\gamma}_z(1)]$$
where $\widetilde{\gamma}_z:[0,1]\rightarrow \mathbb C^\times$ is the unique lift of $\gamma:\pi_1^{\mathrm{top}}(\mathbb C^\times,w_0)$ with $\widetilde{\gamma}_z(0)=z$. 
More generally, let $\Pi_1(X)$ denote the fundamental groupoid of a topological space $X:\mathsf{Top}$, that is, the category whose objects are points of $X$ and whose morphisms are homotopy classes of paths, with concatenation as composition. We have a functor
$$T: \Pi_1(\mathbb C^\times)\rightarrow \mathsf{Set}\quad w\mapsto \mathcal F_{w}\quad \gamma\mapsto T_\gamma$$
whose mapping on morphisms is given by the \textit{transport map}
$$T_\gamma:\mathcal F_{\gamma(0)}\rightarrow\mathcal F_{\gamma(1)}\quad z\mapsto \widetilde{\gamma}_z(1)$$
where $\widetilde{\gamma}_z:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb C^\times$ is the unique lift of $\gamma:[0,1]\rightarrow \mathbb C^\times$ with $\widetilde{\gamma}_z(0)=z$. 

The sheaf $\mathcal F$ is locally constant but not constant: a global section would determine a germ at $w_0$ fixed by all monodromy, whereas transport once around $0$ exchanges the two square-root germs, as demonstrated in \cref{par:square-root-monodromy}. Thus $\mathcal F(\mathbb C^\times)=\emptyset$.
Locally constant (but not necessarily constant) sheaves of sets, such as this one, are known as \textit{local systems (of sets)}.

We have looked at permutation monodromy, but permutation actions do not capture all the situations we wish to study, since a lot of times in nature we encounter actions on vector spaces instead of sets. 
For example, instead of an algebraic equation like the one in \cref{par:square-root-monodromy}, we consider a differential equation, which is known as \textit{Gauss's hypergeometric equation}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gauss-hypergeometric}
\lambda(1-\lambda)\frac{\mathrm d^2 f}{\mathrm d\lambda^2}
+\bigl(c-(a+b+1)\lambda\bigr)\frac{\mathrm d f}{\mathrm d\lambda}
-abf=0
\end{equation}
where we have fixed parameters $a,b,c:\mathbb C$ and an unknown holomorphic function $f:V\rightarrow\mathbb C$ where $V:\mathsf{Open}(U)$ and $U=\mathbb P^1(\mathbb C)\setminus \{0,1,\infty\}$. 
We then define the sheaf of solutions to this equation $\mathcal L:\mathsf{Sh}(U,\mathsf{Vect}_{\mathbb C})$
\[
\mathcal L(V)=\left\{f:\mathcal O(V)\;\middle|\;f\text{ satisfies \eqref{eq:gauss-hypergeometric} on }V\right\}.
\]
This is a sheaf of vector spaces since \eqref{eq:gauss-hypergeometric} is a homogeneous linear differential equation. For each $u:U$, the existence-and-uniqueness theorem \cite[Theorem~4.3.1]{HottaTakeuchiTanisaki2008} gives a sufficiently small open disc $V:\mathsf{Open}(U)$ containing $u$ and an isomorphism of sheaves
\[
\underline{\mathbb C}_V^2\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal L|_V
\]
Thus $\mathcal L$ is locally constant of rank $2$. We call such a sheaf a \textit{complex local system of rank $2$}.
